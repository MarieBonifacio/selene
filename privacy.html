Selene and your data

Privacy policy of Selene (website, Android, iOS and Windows apps). Last updated: 3 October 2026 (“Taking back control” out of the public offer, no more syncing; device backups; error log). En français.

The short version: Selene sells nothing, measures nothing, shows no advertising and has no trackers. What you write in it stays on your device, and on a server only if you create an account, so you can find it on your other devices.

Who is responsible

Selene is published by Marie Bonifacio, developer (a natural person). She is the controller of your data within the meaning of the GDPR: every question or request goes to her, at mariebonifacio.pro@gmail.com.

The website is hosted by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., United States), which logs the IP address of every visit for its security, under its own policy. The apps do not depend on it: they contain the whole of Selene.

Without an account

Everything stays on your device: in the browser (IndexedDB) for the website, in the app's private folder for the apps. Nothing is sent to Selene. The keys you enter (OpenAlex, Zotero, calendar address) are kept on the device, in the system keychain for the apps.

With an account

  • What is kept: your email address and your password (hashed, by Supabase Auth), and your dashboard (tasks, notes, modules, settings, including the place chosen for the sky; of a “Taking back control” tracker, its name only, see below) for synchronisation. Nothing else.
  • Where: at Supabase, the database host, in its Paris (France) data centre, in a table where each account can only read its own row. The server functions (assistant, relay, account deletion) run at Supabase, in the data centre closest to you.
  • The Anthropic API key, if you turn on the assistant: checked, encrypted (AES-GCM) and tied to your account on the server; it never comes back into the page. Your questions to the assistant, with what you chose to let it read, go to Anthropic on behalf of your key; Selene's server keeps neither the questions nor the answers.
  • The relay (reading feeds and pages the browser cannot read on its own) fetches the addresses you give it and keeps nothing.
  • App errors, unless you turn off sending them (Settings → Account): the type of a programming error, the place in the code, the screen, the version and the platform. Neither your account nor anything you write: they are anonymous, kept 30 days at Supabase, to fix Selene.

Taking back control: health data

This space lets you track tobacco, cannabis, alcohol or time spent on social media yourself. What is recorded in it (amounts, cravings, actions, goals, notes) is health data. Since 3 October 2026, it is no longer offered to the public: the publisher keeps it for her personal use, and a tracker created before that date stays open.

  • Where: on your device only; your account keeps only its name. Selene sends its content nowhere and no longer syncs any tracker; only your device's backup (Google, iCloud), if you turned it on, may include it, under your Google or Apple account. A tracker synced before that date, with your consent, stays synced until you keep it on a device, which Selene offers each time you open it; its content then leaves the server.
  • What for: showing it to you, on your devices, and nothing else. No advertising, no statistics, no resale, no cross-analysis with the rest of your dashboard (search, reviews, motifs).
  • The assistant: nothing is sent to it unless you choose to share this tracker. It then receives, with every question, the tracker's name and subject and a summary in numbers (days recorded, totals, goal), never your notes, cravings or triggers. Stopping sharing does not erase what has already been sent.
  • Keeping or erasing: it is included in your full backups and can be exported on its own; deleting the space erases it from your device, then from your account at the next sync.

The services Selene calls for you

Only when you use the feature concerned, directly from your device:

  • Open-Meteo (weather and place search: the coordinates or the name of the chosen place);
  • Crossref, OpenAlex, Zotero (bibliographic references: what you search for, and your Zotero key if you give one);
  • MusicBrainz and Cover Art Archive (records: what you search for);
  • Microlink (preview of a link you save: the address of the link);
  • OpenAgenda, through Opendatasoft's public portal (cultural radar: the rounded area and the dates, never your words, if you use it).

The typefaces are served by Selene itself: displaying them sends your IP address to no third party.

The device's location is only requested if you press “My location” for the home sky. It is immediately rounded to a tenth of a degree (about ten kilometres), kept in your settings like a place chosen by hand (so synchronised with your account, if you have one) and sent to Open-Meteo for the weather. Nothing more precise leaves the device.

Notifications

The mobile apps' morning summary is scheduled by the phone itself: no notification goes through a server.

Why, and on what basis

The GDPR asks to state, for each use, what allows it:

  • Your account and its synchronisation (email address, hashed password, dashboard, encrypted assistant key): the service you ask for by creating an account (performance of a contract, article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • A “Taking back control” tracker still synced (from before 3 October 2026): your explicit, dated consent (article 9(2)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time; the tracker then goes back to a single device.
  • The assistant, the relay and the services called for you: your request, when you use the feature (article 6(1)(b)).
  • Security (technical logs, limited number of attempts): the legitimate interest in protecting the service and the accounts (article 6(1)(f)).
  • The error log: the legitimate interest in fixing Selene's defects (article 6(1)(f)). You can object by turning off sending.

No automated decisions, no profiling, no advertising.

How long

  • Your account and your dashboard: as long as your account exists. Deleting it erases them from the server at once.
  • Your assistant key: until you remove it or delete your account.
  • Database backups: erased data may remain in them for 30 days at most, until they are replaced.
  • Technical logs (IP address, date, request): 1 to 7 days at Supabase, depending on the plan; at GitHub, under its own policy.
  • The error log: 30 days.
  • An email sent to the contact address: as long as it takes to reply, then one year at most after the last exchange.

Outside the European Union

  • Your account stays in the Union: the database is in Paris. Supabase, Inc. is an American company; its data processing agreement governs its role as a processor.
  • The website is served by GitHub, in the United States: your IP address goes through it on every visit.
  • The assistant: your questions go to Anthropic, in the United States, under your own key and your own contract with Anthropic.
  • The services called for you: several are based in the United States (Crossref, OpenAlex, Zotero, MusicBrainz, Cover Art Archive). They only receive your search, from your device.
  • The server functions run in the Supabase data centre closest to you: in Europe if you are there, elsewhere otherwise. They keep nothing.

Your rights

  • Export: Settings → Backup → Export, your whole state in one file.
  • Delete: Settings → Account → Delete my account. Your account, your dashboard on the server and your assistant key are erased at once, then the device is emptied.
  • Without an account: uninstalling the app, or clearing the website's data in the browser, erases everything.
  • Everything else (access, rectification, restriction, objection, withdrawing a consent): write to mariebonifacio.pro@gmail.com from your account's address, which shows the request comes from you. Reply within one month.
  • Complaint: if the reply does not satisfy you, you can refer the matter to the CNIL (in French), the French data protection authority.
